## Quick answer: the AI trends 2027 will bring

**The AI trends 2027 will bring are less about new magic and more about control: AI agents become managed systems with their own identities, AI learns to handle voice and numbers properly, and the quality of your data decides who gets results.** In Europe, add a fourth one: the push to keep data and AI tools under your own control. If you prepare for those four in the first quarter, you'll be ahead of most of your competitors.

We've written this for business leaders who don't follow every product launch. It builds on our guide to [AI agents for business](/en/blog/ai-agents-for-business), and every number below links to its source.

Our main source is Gartner, the research firm whose trend list most CIOs read. On 20 October 2026 it published its list, the [Gartner technology trends 2027](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-20-gartner-identifies-the-top-strategic-technology-trends-for-2027). Some items, like computers made of living cells, are years away from your budget. We picked the ones that touch a normal company's finance, operations, sales and marketing in 2027.

One number for scale. Gartner [forecasts worldwide IT spending of $7.35 trillion in 2027](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-21-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-it-spending-to-grow-15-percent-in-2027-totaling-7-point-35-trillion), up 15% on 2026, with AI infrastructure and AI software driving most of the growth. The question is whether any of it shows up in your own P&L.

## Agents move from pilots to governed platforms

An **AI agent** is software that uses an AI model to carry out a task in several steps on its own: read an invoice, look up the order, check the numbers, draft the entry. 2025 and 2026 were the years of agent pilots. Agentic AI in 2027 is about which pilots get turned into something you can trust at scale, and which get shut down. That question, more than any new model, decides the future of AI agents in your company.

Gartner has been blunt about the second group. In June 2025 it predicted that [over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027), because of rising costs, unclear business value or weak risk controls. The same release estimated that only about 130 of the thousands of vendors selling "agentic AI" are the real thing.

The long-term direction still points up. In that same release, Gartner expects at least 15% of day-to-day work decisions to be made by AI agents by 2028, up from zero in 2024, and a third of enterprise software to include agents by then.

What does a "governed platform" look like? The 2027 list points to two changes.

**Agents get their own identity.** Many agents still run on a shared account, or on an employee's own login. Gartner warns that by 2028, a quarter of enterprise security breaches will trace back to misused AI agents, because of unmanaged machine access and old-style passwords. The fix is to treat each agent like a new employee: its own login, rights that match its job, short-lived access instead of permanent keys, and a log of everything it did. We cover the details in [AI agent security risks](/en/blog/ai-agent-security).

**Agents get a supervisor for their own work.** Gartner points to a growing problem in software teams: AI writes code faster than people can check it, so the checking eats the time the AI saved. The same thing happens in finance and operations. If your agent drafts 500 supplier emails a day and someone has to read every one, you've moved the work, not removed it. A governed agent has automatic checks built in, and it only sends a person the cases that need judgement.

Here's how the shift looks for a typical accounts payable agent:

| | Pilot in 2026 | Governed agent in 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Login | Shared or borrowed account | Its own identity with rights limited to its job |
| Access to the ERP | Broad, "to make the demo work" | Read orders, create drafts, no payments |
| Checking its work | A person reviews everything | Automatic checks, people review the exceptions |
| Cost tracking | Nobody knows the monthly bill | Budget and step limits per task |
| Exit | Built inside one vendor's tool | Documented, with data you can export |

If you want to see how an agent connects to your systems with rights like these, read [how to connect an AI agent to your ERP and CRM](/en/blog/connect-ai-agent-erp-crm). For the cost side, see [AI agent token cost](/en/blog/ai-agent-token-cost).

## AI beyond text: voice and numbers

Most of what businesses call AI today is text in, text out. Two of the emerging AI trends Gartner highlights push past that, and both matter for ordinary companies.

**Voice that reasons.** Older voice bots turn speech into text, run it through a script, then read an answer back. That's why they lose the thread when a caller changes topic halfway. Gartner describes a new kind of model that works on the audio itself and keeps track of what the caller wants across the whole conversation. Its analyst put the business case simply: "Voice remains the enterprise's highest-volume interaction channel."

Don't rip out your call centre. Pick one call type with clear rules, like delivery status or appointment changes, and test whether a voice agent can resolve it fully, with a clean handover to a person when it can't. In MENA and Africa, test it in the languages and accents your customers actually use, including dialect, before you trust any vendor's demo.

**Models built for numbers.** General chat models are good with language and they're unreliable with arithmetic, because they predict likely answers instead of calculating them. Gartner names a separate class, large quantitative models, built to work on numerical data for forecasting and simulation. Think demand planning, cash-flow forecasting or pricing scenarios.

For a CFO, the lesson holds even now: don't let a chat model invent your numbers. Today the safe design is to let the AI write the question and let a calculation engine, your ERP or a spreadsheet model produce the figure. A finance assistant that drafts the board commentary from a forecast your systems computed is useful. One that computes the forecast itself is a risk.

## Data quality becomes the bottleneck

This is the trend we'd underline twice. Every agent, voice bot and forecast above depends on one thing, and it isn't the model. It's whether the AI can find the right information and understand what it means.

Gartner calls the fix an "AI context platform": a layer that gives agents the business knowledge they need to read inputs and act in line with your goals. In plainer words, it's an organised, permission-aware memory of how your company works. Gartner predicts that by 2027, organisations that put the meaning of their data first will [raise the accuracy of their agents by up to 80% and cut costs by up to 60%](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-20-gartner-identifies-the-top-strategic-technology-trends-for-2027).

"Meaning" is the important word. Here's an illustrative example. Your sales team says "active customer" and means anyone who bought in the last 12 months. Finance means anyone with an open contract. Ask an agent "how many active customers do we have?" and it'll pick one definition at random, or worse, mix them. No model upgrade fixes that. A shared definition, written down once and given to the agent, does.

This is also why we always build in the same order: first sound processes, then clean, structured data, then AI on top. A few checks you can run on your own data this quarter:

- **Definitions.** Do your key terms (customer, order, margin, churn) mean the same thing in every team?
- **Ownership.** Does each important dataset have a named owner who fixes errors?
- **Freshness.** How old is the data the agent would read? A price list from last year gives confident wrong answers.
- **Access.** Can you control which people, and which agents, see which documents?

## Sovereign tech and what it means in Europe

Gartner defines sovereign tech as technology built so you can realistically leave a provider and keep operating. It predicts that by 2030, [over 75% of organisations based outside the US will adopt digital sovereignty strategies](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-20-gartner-identifies-the-top-strategic-technology-trends-for-2027), moving important workloads to reduce geopolitical risk and dependence on a single vendor.

In Europe this is already concrete. Three examples:

- **The EU is funding its own AI computing.** The European Commission's [AI Continent Action Plan](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/ai-continent-action-plan) includes at least 13 operational AI factories by 2026, up to five larger AI gigafactories backed by €20 billion, and a goal to triple the EU's data centre capacity in the next five to seven years.
- **Cloud providers sell EU-only options.** AWS announced in January 2026 the [general availability of its European Sovereign Cloud](https://press.aboutamazon.com/aws/2026/1/aws-launches-aws-european-sovereign-cloud-and-announces-expansion-across-europe), with its first region in Brandenburg, Germany, run independently from its other regions.
- **AI providers offer data residency.** OpenAI lets [API customers process data in Europe for eligible endpoints](https://openai.com/index/introducing-data-residency-in-europe/), and new ChatGPT Enterprise and Edu customers can have their content stored in Europe.

Regulation adds a deadline. The EU AI Act's [Digital Omnibus amendment](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/ai-act-explorer/digital-omnibus/) entered into force on 27 July 2026. It moved the obligations for high-risk AI listed in Annex III, which covers uses like [hiring, credit scoring and access to essential services](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/annex-3), to 2 December 2027. Rules for AI inside regulated products listed in Annex I follow on 2 August 2028. If you're planning an agent that screens job applicants or scores borrowers, 2027 is your preparation year. Our article on [AI agents, GDPR and the EU AI Act](/en/blog/ai-agents-gdpr-eu-ai-act) explains what each role has to do.

What does sovereignty mean for a mid-sized company? Not building your own data centre. It means three practical habits: know where your data is stored and processed, keep it in formats you can export, and write a one-page exit plan for each critical AI vendor. That last one sounds like paperwork. It's the thing that saves you when prices or terms change overnight.

This matters outside Europe too. A software company in Tunis or Casablanca that serves European clients will be asked these questions in every procurement round. If you're comparing assistants, our comparison of [Copilot vs ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini](/en/blog/copilot-vs-chatgpt-claude-gemini) covers where each one stores your data.

## What stays hard

Trend lists make everything sound close. Some things won't get easier in 2027.

**Choosing the right problem.** Gartner's own advice from 2025 still holds: use agents where a decision is needed, plain automation for routine steps and simple assistants for lookups. Plenty of "agent" projects are a workflow rule in disguise, and the rule is cheaper.

**Hardware costs.** Gartner's spending forecast notes that data centre demand has [more than doubled memory prices over the past 12 months](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-21-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-it-spending-to-grow-15-percent-in-2027-totaling-7-point-35-trillion), and it expects that pressure to last through 2027. Laptops, servers and some cloud services will cost more.

**People and process.** An agent that removes three hours of work a day helps only if someone redesigns the job around the freed time. That's management work, and no vendor ships it in the box.

**Measuring results.** Many companies still can't say what their AI pilots saved. If you can't measure the before, you can't prove the after.

## Your Q1 2027 checklist

Here's what we'd put on a leadership agenda for January to March. None of it needs a big budget.

| # | Action | Owner | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | List your AI pilots and decide: scale, fix or stop | CEO or COO | Each pilot has a decision and a measurable target |
| 2 | Pick one or two workflows with a clear cost or time problem | Operations and finance | The baseline (hours, errors, cost) is measured |
| 3 | Write shared definitions for your ten most-used business terms | Data owner per team | Sales, finance and operations sign them off |
| 4 | Give every AI agent its own login with limited rights | IT or security | No agent uses a shared or personal account |
| 5 | Set a monthly AI budget and a step limit per agent | CFO | Spend is visible every month |
| 6 | Write a one-page exit plan for each critical AI vendor | CIO or IT lead | You know where the data lives and how to export it |
| 7 | Check planned uses against the EU AI Act high-risk list | Legal or compliance | High-risk uses have a plan for December 2027 |
| 8 | Test one voice or document use case in your customers' languages | Customer service lead | Results measured on real calls or files |

## FAQ

### What are the biggest AI trends for 2027?

For most businesses, four things matter: AI agents moving from experiments to managed platforms with their own identities and permissions, AI that works with voice and numbers instead of only text, data quality becoming the main limit on results, and a push in Europe toward technology you can leave or move. Gartner's list for 2027 covers all four.

### Are AI agents ready for production in 2027?

For well-chosen tasks, yes. Gartner still expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be canceled by the end of 2027, mostly over cost, unclear value or weak risk controls. The projects that survive pick one measurable workflow, give the agent narrow permissions and keep a person on the decisions that carry risk.

### What is sovereign tech?

Sovereign tech is technology built so that you can realistically leave a provider: your data, models and processes stay usable if a vendor changes terms, raises prices or becomes unavailable. In practice it means data residency, open formats, a written exit plan and not tying every process to one vendor.

### When do the EU AI Act high-risk rules apply?

After the Digital Omnibus amendment, which entered into force on 27 July 2026, the obligations for high-risk AI systems listed in Annex III, such as hiring or credit scoring, apply from 2 December 2027. Those for AI built into products covered by Annex I apply from 2 August 2028.

### What should a company do about AI in Q1 2027?

Pick one or two workflows with a clear cost or time problem, check the quality of the data behind them, give every AI agent its own login with limited rights, write down how you'd leave your main AI vendor, and check whether any planned use falls under the EU AI Act's high-risk categories.
